How it works

Your message never leaves your device. The whole checker runs inside your browser. Nothing you paste is sent anywhere or saved.

Second Look uses a machine learning model trained on about 5,000 real text messages from two public research datasets, each labeled as normal, marketing spam, or a scam. It learned which words and patterns show up in scams, like "claim," "verify," or "account suspended."

On top of that, it checks every link and the message itself for warning signs that scammers rely on: web addresses pretending to be a real company, link shorteners that hide where you'll end up, pressure to act fast, and requests for gift cards or account codes. If it spots a serious one, it tells you to be careful even if the wording seems normal.

Tested on 851 messages it had never seen, it warned about 91% of scams and wrongly warned about only 0.6% of normal texts.

What it can miss

  • Friendly sounding scams with no link, like "Hi Mom, this is my new number" or a surprise job offer. Those depend on context the model can't see.
  • Brand new tricks. Most of the training messages are a few years old, so the warning sign checks do a lot of the work on newer scams.
  • It's built for text messages. Long emails may not score as accurately.

No checker is perfect. If a message pressures you, asks for money, or asks you to log in, go to the company's real app or website yourself instead of using anything in the message.
